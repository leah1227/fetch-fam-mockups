  • Tools that live in the app — clicker, whistle, timer
  • Streak dots and a training passport

What's deliberately different

The file is read before the quiz is asked. Woofz opens cold with 10–15 questions. Fetch opens by showing what it already holds — three claims, a breed, an age — and what each one means for training. The quiz still happens, because claims data is blind to behaviour, but three of the six questions are confirmations rather than interrogation.

The blind spot is stated out loud. Pulling, barking, jumping up and separation distress never generate a claim, so the app says so and asks. Use the Switch control on the file screen to see the same flow for a dog with no claims — the seeded questions disappear and the quiz gets longer.

Two progress bars. His skills and yours. The bond is the product, so the owner is on the chart.

Levels are freedoms, not tricks. Off lead at the park, asleep under a café table — things you do together, not commands he performs.

The record is a keepsake. Private moments with dates. Nothing posted, ranked or compared.

Experts are named and dated. A real person, a real start date, a Sunday check-in — no cohort chat, no leaderboard.
